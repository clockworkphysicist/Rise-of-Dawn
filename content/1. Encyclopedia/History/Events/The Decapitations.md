# Event

## History

The Decapitations were a two-hour span of terror and confusion that struck the city of Bravlatsa during the reign of King Janis Ravalcovic. The crisis followed weeks of mounting tension between the king and his court over disputed authority of a border town. Then, without warning or discernible cause, the people of Bravlatsa turned on one another. Witnesses described men, women, and children suddenly seizing knives, axes, and any edge of iron they could grasp, and—without expression or hesitation—severing the heads of neighbors, kin, and friends. Some even set upon themselves, tearing their own lives away in grotesque self-decapitations.

Panic erupted in the royal castle. Many assumed a hex had been cast over the city, and King Janis immediately summoned his court wizard to uncover the nature of the affliction. Yet before a single spell of scrying could be prepared, the frenzy ended as abruptly as it had begun. Citizens dropped their weapons and resumed their daily routines as though nothing had transpired. By then, hundreds of bodies lay in the streets, their blood still fresh on the cobbles.

Interrogations yielded little. Most survivors appeared confused by the questions put to them. Some were dimly aware that something unusual had occurred, while others seemed wholly ignorant, denying any involvement at all. Few displayed sorrow or remorse.

Though the court’s wizard scoured every art he knew, no explanation was uncovered beyond vague suspicion of witchcraft. To this day, no firm cause has been established. Modern scholars tend to frame the incident as an unprecedented episode of stress-induced mass hysteria. Yet the sheer violence and uniformity of the Decapitations remain a mystery that continues to unnerve even the most skeptical historians.
