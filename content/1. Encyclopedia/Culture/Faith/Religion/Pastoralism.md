# Pastoralism

## History

The history of the Pastoral Religion, and its mythology, can be best understood through then lens of its three 'primary' saints which they believe establish the Church it operates as.

The church itself was founded by Saint Mary the Prophet, who was born with the gift of foresight. However, in her foresight, she saw prophecy as though being given words from God himself; who she identified as ontological good and the essence of all grace. Mary claimed that God was the father of reality, and that he created man, all forms of man, in his image and that it was mankind's purpose and meaning to love others and bring all things towards God. She prophesized the coming of a time where all of mankind would be brought to this goal, and that on this day the heavens would open up and become one with the earth.

She was, in effect, a miracle worker, lending credence to her words. She had no magic, no abilities, and yet she was able to do things which most all forms of magic would have been impossible for, including raising the dead. However, she also foresaw her own death, and as she had seen, it came to pass; the community she was in rejected her, and she was lynched under the very sycamore tree she taught and preached under.

Years later came Saint Alexis, the Holy Martyr. She was six years old when she became the high priestess of the last remaining Pastoral community. Under the tyranny of Agadain, Alexis preached publicly for six years, teaching people on the nature of God, and how their communities should act to be in his example. She believed that it was the responsibility of Gods faithful to act by the examples of grace and divinity they see in others, and in doing so, become closer to him. However, she also taught people about the importance of spreading this love and extending it through virtues: chastity, temperance, charity, diligence, kindness, patience, and humility. She believed heavily in mercy and forgiveness, recognizing the inherent dignity in all people; seeing God in anyone. However, at age 12, her community was raided by the Agadanians. One by one the members of her community apostatized, until Alexis was the last. She refused to abandon God and so was sentenced to death.

The martyrdom of Saint Alexis was particularly brutal; she was tied to a post, slashed across her abdomen, and when she still refused to apostatize her executioner took a metal nail and with one strike hammered it into her forehead, killing her. The guards then cremated her body so nothing would be left and she couldn't get a proper burial, all that was left being the nail that was embedded in her head.

The man who was once her most vocal detractor, who had begged and pleaded for her to renounce her faith was given the nail. In rage at the cruel world, he threw the nail into the branches of St. Mary's tree and wept. In the midst of crying, there was a flutter and the nail fell out of the tree and hit him on the head as a single white dove flew to the north. It was in that experience he claimed to have seen God, and led Alexis' community from their land to the north, where he promised he would find them a land where they could worship in peace.

This led them to the walls of the Ascalonian Empire.

This leads to Saint Vincent, the first Pontiff. The Pastoralists fled north until they came across the walls of the Empire; cold marble walls which would fall for no man. Believing this to be their punishment for their apostasy and abandoning their God and their Priestess in her last moments, the community prayed, cried out, and bared their hearts, calling out for deliverance. It was then, as the guards continued their patrols uncaringly, an earthquake struck, and for the first time in Ascalons history, a section of the Wall of Lothren crumbled before the faithful, who entered into the empire to the shock and paralyzing bewilderment of the guards. The pastoralists set up a camp in the valley, trying to nestle a humble home they could call their own, but news spread quickly to the capital, and where his Arch-General advised that the intruders be crushed and the problem be dealt with, Vincent was principled, wise, he wished to know the nature of the intrusion and, using the legal system in place, have the intruders arrested and exiled.

In order to account for and enumerate their crimes, he sent his own personal advisor and secretary to audit them, however he did not return. This caused a state of alarm for the Emperor, who now had a personal friend who may have been lost to violent revolt, and so gathered a segment of the army to accompany him as he performed an inquisition against the Pastoralists. However, to his shock, he found that not only was his friend alive, he had joined them. In confusion and bewilderment, he had decided to talk with his advisor with latent hope of swaying him back to his side, and also to understand what had happened. Upon hearing of the community leader, the man who led the flight from Agadain, Leonard, Vincent demanded to speak with him. What followed was days of discussion, debate, philosophy, and on the final day the Emperor gave him before he passed his judgement, Leonard gifted Vincent with the nail that had killed Alexis.

Vincent was shocked by this gift, the most important thing their community had. Leonard, however, rebuked him; it was not the most important thing they had.

This gesture is believed to be what swayed the Emperor, and so, he gave them emittance, and allowed them to stay only so long as he would be allowed to personally oversee and supervise them, making regular visits to make his judgements on their continued existence. As years passed, he became closer to them, and the debates and discussions which Leonard would have with Vincent became public talks on matters of philosophy, theology, rhetoric, history, the logical conclusions and contingents of the values and virtues they held. People were amazed by the emperor, seeing him as a man who was graced with divine logic and ability towards reason none of them had considered as part of divinity before, but certainly after. Eventually, Leonard, a human, passed from old age, and the community found itself without a leader to protect and look after it. The only man who had given them a legal contract with the imperial structure was gone. Knowing this, however, Vincent spoke and asked their people who among them would best lead their community within Ascalon. All of whom then pointed to him. Throughout the years, they had decided their identity and their communities nature; they were Ascalonians. Pastoralist Ascalonians, and so, the were loyal to their God and their Emperor. Vincent was shocked at this perspective, and, perhaps through movement of faith or quiet embrace, he accepted; informally at first, but then formally allowing the Pastoral Religion to exist freely in the Empire. From there it spread, as Vincent used the knowledge he gained and the perspectives he had learned about to bolster his Empire and help build up an administration who could support the Pastoralists and help them integrate. He would seemingly write nonstop on theology, writing thousands of tomes as he created what became known as 'scripture'.
He had become known to the Pastoralists as a father figure for their faith, and so with the Ascalonian tongue, gave him a title; Pontifex Maximus.

It is these three saints that the Church establishes it's hagiographic tradition on, and holds to the Church as fulfilling the three roles by which its establishment is meant to act as:

Priest, Prophet, and King
