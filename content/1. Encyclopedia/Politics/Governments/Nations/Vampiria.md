## Culture and Traditions

Vampires can most commonly be seen wearing their veils. The veil covering their mouths is because, for them, mouths are sacred. They are the vehicles of the minds presence in the world (speech), they are the flow of life (breath), and they are the means by which one replenishes their lifeforce (eating). The mouth also is the universal key to the truest and most intimate depths of a persons heart, and acts as the way that lifeforce is passed on from person to person, generation to generation (drinking blood).

The Vampires believe, because of this, that life is like a song; one which they sing with their continued living and by being one with the symphony. To them, the moon acts as a conductor of sorts, preparing them for the next verse.

There are four situations where removing ones veil is expected.

1. A kiss: It's seen as being too distant, cold, and inauthentic. Only exception is for a dead person.
2. Singing the end of season song
3. During the blood moon. Wearing a veil during the bloodmoon is seen as a twisted blasphemy in their culture, but that said this time is one where there are no consequences for anything, so it's more of a "this is horrible, but if it happens we understand you didn't have control"
4. Drinking blood. You must take off the veil entirely when you do this.

When removing the veil, it's a matter of honor, mostly. As for specific things to do, brushing it to the side is the most "modest" way of doing so, with the most intimate being to remove it entirely. There is no punishment for keeping it on, maybe social stigmatization and everyone acting like they can't hear you for a while.

Taking it off inappropriately has four levels of punishment depending on the context.

In terms of severity:

1. Forced nudity for a set amount of time
2. Muzzling for a set amount of time
3. Pulling teeth
4. Condemnation to the briar patch

First severity is immodesty. If one wishes to be immodest by removing their veil, then their clothes will go with it.

Second is a violation of the song of life. In contrast to the first, this isn't for humiliation by laying everything bare, it's punishment by restricting ones access to the sanctity and dignity of that which gives life.

Third is punishment by pain, pulling a single tooth.

Last one is, in 75% of cases, a death sentence. Taken up to a cliff above the briar patches of the eastern lands, with ones teeth pulled, their clothes removed, and their mouth muzzled, and tossed down into the thorns. In most cases, they are either pricked to death, they land on a giant thorn and are impaled, or they die from exposure and starvation in the endless maze of spikes and thorns. Some do escape, and if they do, they can rejoin society, but usually as a wretch begging for whatever they can get through the holes in their muzzle.

Vampirics, for centuries, had to fight off the demons and Voranians; and they were damn good at it. They would usually drink the blood from their dead, but this had it's own risk of corruption. So, often, they would drink from eachother; the counts keeping a watchful eye on who has or hasn't given blood to ensure that when the Blood Moon comes there will be an effective response. Days before it arrives, signs start appearing. Womens voices may suddenly hike up in pitch like they've suddenly sucked in helium and it won't go back down, the deep red color of the vampires hair will become bright red, their teeth will start to sharpen, their eyes will glow red in the dark, their limbs elongate, their hands grow, and they may suddenly find themselves uncontrollably laughing. It is when this time comes that all of the children are guided by the older children on a pilgrimage to defensive vaults in the mountains, where they will be safe and protected by the harlequins and the snarevines until their parents return to their senses.
