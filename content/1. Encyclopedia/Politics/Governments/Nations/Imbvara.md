## Religion

Gnomic faith practices a form of Totemism relating to a direct connection to spirits that are manifested through different animals. These spirits are overseen by a sort of “Spirit of Light” which connects all life, which is embodied by the High Matriarch of Nawenyanak’. They believe that the High Matriarch acts as a living sacrifice to the sun, and is responsible for appeasing it twice every year around Solar Equinox by offering up some of her spirit and soul at the temple. The High Matriarch is chosen from one of the tribes depending on what animal is decided to be demanded to be sacrificed to the sun, which the Shaman Monks of the Hana’kewa decide based on signs they perceive during blood sacrifices at the various totems. It is believed that the sun will demand a virgin sacrifice, one which has not and does not bond with anyone and has no attachment to the world in accordance with Menwa’patha, or the practice of rejecting attachment and desire.

## Culture and Traditions

Menwa’patha is the gnomic practice of asceticism by which one is meant to divest themselves of attachment and desire. By this practice, Gnomes are expected to treat concepts of ownership as minimally as possible, and are guided to live modestly. This fits in as a cultural response with their strong emotional and social attachment instinct, which can potentially be fatal if broken. Because of this reason, Gnomic Shamans and Matriarchs are expected to be completely celibate, cultivating this sense of detachment and practicing it in deference to the will of the spirits.
