# Agadain

## History

Aleoren and Talaexora were once one Empire, Agadain. The Agadanian Triarchy was an Empire that consisted by a rule of three Basileus, Philius, Menarion, and Naecentios; The Lord of the Autumn, Lord of the River, and Lord of the Hills and Dale. These three kings, despite their different patron gods, unified and rose up against the Oreyenic Empire to the south, forming a strong bulwark of power that they claimed was the will of the gods made manifest. Victory after victory almost seemed to prove this, as Agadain not only rose up against Oreyen, but conquered it entirely. For 200 years, Agadain looted Oreyen and used it's remaining wealth to enrich their empire, building temples to the gods in reverence and to ensure their power would be maintained. As they expanded eastward past the Sindaric river all the way to the Nexonian Rift, the Agadanians had taken the recently liberated Lunaria and handily crushed it, destroying the capitol city of Lunavale and reducing the Lunarians to a shattered and near extinct race.

This came to an end, however, when Legatus Gaius Flavius of the Ascalonian Empire, in a bid to make a name for himself to the Imperial Court, launched an invasion of the land that Agadain conquered west of the Sendaric river in the name of imperial conquest. The resuting war saw Agadain experience a strong series of victories that even caused Ascalon to lose ground, but then quickly saw the Agadanians experiencing heavy losses. This attrition eventually caused Agadain to experience their first loss, which was not only one sided, but near humiliating. Quickly losing morale and being thrown into a panic at their first ever failure, the Agadanian Army fell into a death spiral where they simply experienced loss after loss. Eventually, the war was decided at the battle of Phos-Hilaron, in which the Ascalonian Army, supported by the Oreyenic rebels, ousted the Agadanians for good.

With a treaty coming swiftly after, Agadain experienced an internal conflict in which the Three Basileus blamed eachother for their failures, losing the gods favor and bringing calamity upon their empire. The three kings went to war, until all that was left was Basileus Naecentios' son, Narcencion Dinopolyus, who made an agreement with the daughter of the Lord of Autumn to allow his former demesne to remain in her families hands. Sacrificing 1000 sheep and cattle to the gods at Pneumenaktalon Temple for their favor in choosing him, Narcencion began to eye the prize that was lost centuries ago, the lands of Oreyen, knowing they would be the first step to getting revenge on their now hated enemies, the Ascalonians.

However, it had become obvious that Oreyen was no longer the worthy opponent it once was, and not wanting to experience the immediate ire of the now bordering Ascalonian Empire, he decided to attack the, at the time, politically isolated new Exalic kingdom which once made up Oreyens southern lands; Varaje. The war was a complete and utter failure, ending only three years after it was declared with, seemingly, no military action taken. Spending decades after rebuilding after the bandit attacks which plagued the lands, Agadain was relatively quiet for many years, barring the odd skirmish against their neighbors here and there. It wasn't until the ill fated "War of Ashen Blood" that Agadain saw it's chance at revenge against the Ascalonians, and waged a brutal all out war over the Sendaric river. The war, however, ended in yet another crushing defeat, with Agadain not even managing to hold a single city they had conquered. In a fit of anger at the gods for once again spurning them, the king of Agadain ordered the statues of the gods in Navarra defaced, causing the shock and horror of many.
After many years of war, defeat, and desolation, public unrest reached a fever pitch, with many blaming the royal family for losing the favor of the gods. Though outspoken critics of the royal family would often be silenced, an influential clergyman by the name Constantios Eliopoluos called for the religious faithful in Agadain to abscond the kingdom, and to act under a new form of democracy. Realizing that the influence of the high priests had become too much of a problem, the Royal court began a series of trials against offenders, eventually leading to scores of religious devotees acting in outright revolt against the kingdom. Though information on this period is somewhat murky, the events that occurred shortly after a response to the protests was known as the “Theophilian Massacre.” This would lead into the Agadanian civil war. Agadain was well into the throes of full civil war with the north, with multiple Ascalonian war-lords supporting the south and multiple human kingdoms supporting the north.

Though the war was brutal, and the casualties many, the North eventually won the war, as well as their independence. During the signing of the White-Blue Treaty, created by a mixed Human-Exalic council, Agadain was split between Talexora in the north and Aleoren in the south, with Aleoren and her allies officially promising not to act in aggression against the new democratic kingdom. After these events, Aleoren officially declared its stance that the gods should be destroyed, and that any citizen who still acted in faith towards any declared divine being would face the full penalty of law. After these events, there was a long period of moderate peace between Talexora and Aleoren, with the two nations directing their efforts inward towards the further development of their nations. Near the end of the second era, Queen Dulsa shockingly and completely vanished from court life, leaving her regent to rule over the land until her elder son came to power. However, upon claiming the throne, the young man abdicated and went into self exile, ordering his younger brother Mencius, only of age 6, to hold the throne. This event would come to be known as the Tekemnenos affair.

Basileus Mencius took to the throne with strong nationalistic zeal, and as the boy became a man, he instituted hardline laws in support of increased personal autonomy amongst the individual nations within the Exalic Union. Four years before the third era, Basileus Mencius called to order the Exalic Council, sans the Ascalonian Empire, and gave the “Speech of Aganestia”, calling for peace and co-operation for all of the Exalic nations. Fifteen years after the beginning of the third era, however, Aleoren broke the White-Blue treaty during Talexora’s general election season, throwing the relations between the Exalic Union and the Human Confederacy into a new cold war. As Aleorenic soldiers marched on Talexora, they were able to deftly and efficiently play the two political sides against one another, leading to a quick and decisive victory. A few months after Aleoren occupied the area, and under the decree of Basileus Mencius Dinopolyus, elections will continue as planned, though now for head administrator, and the two states may yet find themselves unified as Agadain once again.
